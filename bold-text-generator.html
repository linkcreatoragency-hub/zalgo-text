Sans bold or serif bold?

Sans Bold (𝗕𝗼𝗹𝗱) matches the interface font of nearly every app and social platform, so it looks like genuine bold formatting rather than a decorative swap. This is the default choice.

Serif Bold (𝐁𝐨𝐥𝐝) has traditional bracketed serifs. It stands out more, which is useful when you want the styling to be noticed as styling.

Both include bold digits, which many other Unicode styles do not — handy for prices, dates and stats.

Why not just use markdown?

Where real formatting is available, use it. Discord's **bold**, Slack's, Reddit's and WhatsApp's *bold* all produce proper bold text that screen readers announce correctly and search engines can index.

Unicode bold is for the many places with no formatting at all: Instagram bios and captions, TikTok profiles, X display names, YouTube titles, LinkedIn headlines, form fields and plain-text notes. In those places it is the only way to get emphasis.

Rule of thumb: if the app has a bold button or markdown, use it. If it does not, use these characters.

Using emphasis well

  • Bold the first two or three words of a long caption so the preview line carries the hook.
  • Bold one number. A single 𝟯𝟬% in plain text is far more effective than a bold sentence.
  • Use it for labels — 𝗪𝗵𝗲𝗻: Friday 8pm — in an event post.
  • Do not bold everything. Emphasis is relative; when it is all bold, none of it is.

Things to know before you commit

The trade-offs worth knowing

  • Search engines and site search may not match it. To a computer, 𝓱𝓮𝓵𝓵𝓸 is not the word hello. Keep your searchable text plain.
  • Screen readers handle it badly. Many announce the characters one by one or skip them, so avoid styling text that carries important information.
  • Some fonts lack the glyphs. Where a device has no glyph you get a box (□). Coverage is very good on modern phones and desktops, weaker on older devices and some smart TVs.
  • A few platforms filter it from names. Facebook and some games restrict which characters may appear in a real-name field.

None of that matters for a bio, a caption or a username. All of it matters for body text, headings on your own website, or anything you want found in search.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make bold text on Instagram?

Instagram has no bold button, so type your text above, copy the Sans Bold version, and paste it into your bio or caption.

Is Unicode bold real bold?

The characters are genuinely bold in shape, but they are not formatting. Your text stays a single unformatted string that happens to look bold.

Does bold text work on LinkedIn?

Yes, in headlines and posts. Use it sparingly on a professional profile — it can read as gimmicky, and recruiters' search tools may not match it.

Why can't I search for bold text?

Search engines treat 𝗯𝗼𝗹𝗱 as different characters from “bold”, so styled text is effectively invisible to search. Keep anything searchable plain.

Are there bold numbers?

Yes — the Sans Bold and Serif Bold styles both include 0–9.

Should I use this instead of markdown?

No. Where markdown or a bold button exists, use it; it is better for accessibility and search. Unicode bold is for fields with no formatting at all.
