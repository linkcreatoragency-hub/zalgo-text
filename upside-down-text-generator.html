How flipping actually works

There is no rotate operation in Unicode. What happens instead is a two-step substitution.

  1. Each character is swapped for a lookalike. A rotated a resembles ɐ (a Latin phonetic character), a rotated e resembles ǝ, a rotated t resembles ʇ. These come from phonetic alphabets, Greek, Cyrillic and mathematical symbol blocks — characters designed for entirely different purposes that happen to have the right shape.
  2. The whole string is reversed. Turning a page upside down reverses reading order as well as letter orientation, so the text has to be flipped end to end too.

The result only approximates rotation, which is why a few letters never look quite right.

The letters that do not cooperate

Some shapes have no plausible upside-down partner in Unicode, so every flipper compromises:

LetterFlipped asWhy
hH (unchanged)No inverted h exists; the capital is the least jarring stand-in
s, z, x, o, lunchangedThese are close to symmetrical, so they need no substitute
QΌBorrowed from Greek; the tail sits differently
7ㄥA CJK stroke character — the closest available shape
4ㄣAlso borrowed from CJK strokes
Why some characters show as boxes: the substitutes come from many different Unicode blocks, and not every device font covers all of them. Simple lowercase words flip most reliably.

Things to know before you commit

The trade-offs worth knowing

  • Search engines and site search may not match it. To a computer, 𝓱𝓮𝓵𝓵𝓸 is not the word hello. Keep your searchable text plain.
  • Screen readers handle it badly. Many announce the characters one by one or skip them, so avoid styling text that carries important information.
  • Some fonts lack the glyphs. Where a device has no glyph you get a box (□). Coverage is very good on modern phones and desktops, weaker on older devices and some smart TVs.
  • A few platforms filter it from names. Facebook and some games restrict which characters may appear in a real-name field.

None of that matters for a bio, a caption or a username. All of it matters for body text, headings on your own website, or anything you want found in search.

Frequently asked questions

How do I flip text upside down?

Type it into the box above. Each letter is swapped for an inverted lookalike and the string is reversed so it reads correctly when rotated.

Why do some letters stay the same?

Letters like o, s, x and z look nearly identical when rotated, so no substitution is needed. Others, such as h, have no inverted form in Unicode.

Can I flip it back?

Yes — paste the flipped text into the box and it flips again, returning close to the original. A few substituted characters may not survive the round trip exactly.

Does upside down text work in usernames?

On most platforms yes, though some filter unusual characters out of name fields.

Why do I see boxes instead of letters?

The substitute characters come from several Unicode blocks and your device's font may lack some of them. Shorter, lowercase text flips most reliably.

Is it the same as mirror text?

No. Upside-down text rotates 180 degrees; mirror text reflects horizontally, like text seen in a mirror.
