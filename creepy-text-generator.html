The dripping look, and how to get it

Most Zalgo output is symmetrical: marks climb above the letters and sink below them in roughly equal measure. That reads as chaos. Creepy text reads as decay, and the difference comes down to direction.

Switch off marks above and leave marks below on. The tops of your letters stay clean and readable while everything underneath melts downward, like ink running or something seeping out of the words. It is a small change with a completely different emotional register.

Try this combination: craziness around 40–50, marks above off, marks through on, marks below on. Readable at a glance, unmistakably wrong on a second look.

Ideas for Halloween and horror content

  • Party invitations — put the guest's name in creepy text and leave the address plain so people can actually find the party.
  • Costume reveal captions — a lightly dripping caption sells the photo better than three ghost emoji.
  • Horror game streams — a corrupted stream title stands out in a directory full of clean ones.
  • Story text overlays — screenshot the output and drop it onto footage in your editor; it keeps rendering exactly as generated.
  • Creepypasta and ARG writing — escalate the level paragraph by paragraph so the text itself decays as the story does.
  • Spooky playlists — playlist titles accept Unicode on every major music service.

Creepy text without the wall of marks

Heavy corruption is not the only way to look unsettling, and it is the first thing platforms clip. Some alternatives that survive anywhere:

  • Gothic blackletter — 𝕲𝖔𝖙𝖍𝖎𝖈 𝕿𝖊𝖝𝖙 carries centuries of horror-novel association and stays perfectly legible.
  • Strikethrough — c̶r̶o̶s̶s̶e̶d̶ ̶o̶u̶t̶ words imply something was hidden, which is often creepier than something being loud.
  • Upside-down text — sparingly, one flipped word in a normal sentence is deeply odd.
  • Light Zalgo on one word only — the contrast does the work.

Keeping it fun rather than annoying

Spooky styling works because it is a surprise. It stops working when it is everywhere — and it actively hurts when it lands in the wrong place.

Keep creepy text to captions, thumbnails, titles and one-off posts. Keep it out of display names, work channels and anything a screen reader will announce repeatedly, where dozens of accent names get read aloud for a single word. A little goes a long way, in both directions.

Frequently asked questions

What is creepy text?

Creepy text is ordinary text with Unicode combining marks attached below the letters so they appear to drip or decay — a Halloween-friendly variation on Zalgo.

How do I make text look like it is dripping?

Uncheck “marks above” and keep “marks below” on. All the distortion then falls beneath the baseline.

Can I use creepy text on Instagram?

Yes, in bios and captions. Keep the level moderate because Instagram limits how far marks may overflow a line.

Is there a creepy font I can download instead?

This tool uses Unicode characters, not a font file, which is why it works everywhere without installing anything. For print work a blackletter or horror display font is a better fit.

Does it work for Halloween invitations?

Yes — style the names and headings, but keep dates and addresses in plain text so guests can read them.

How do I get my normal text back?

Paste the result into the Zalgo to normal text tool.
