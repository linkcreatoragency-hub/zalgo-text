Filled or outlined?

Filled squares (🅢🅠🅤🅐🅡🅔) are the striking option — solid blocks with knocked-out letters, close to how a keyboard key or a hazard sign looks. Strong enough to work as a standalone logo line.

Outlined squares (🄾🅄🅃🄻🄸🄽🄴) are lighter and sit more comfortably next to normal text.

Case and digits: both squared sets have a single case, so capitals and lowercase render identically, and there are no squared digits — numbers stay in your normal font.

Where the characters actually come from

These characters were not designed for decoration. They were added to Unicode to represent symbols used in Japanese broadcasting and mobile standards — regional indicators, content-rating badges, and similar signage.

That origin explains two quirks. First, some devices render them in colour, because a few live alongside emoji. Second, a handful of letter combinations may trigger flag rendering on some systems, since paired regional-indicator characters form country flags. If your text suddenly shows a flag, that is why — change a letter or use the outlined set.

Where the style fits

  • Gaming tags and clan names — blocky and aggressive by default.
  • Short headings in a bio — one word, high impact.
  • Warning-style jokes — the visual language of a hazard label.
  • Album and track titles — the industrial look suits electronic music.
  • Category labels in a plain-text list.

Squared letters are very wide, so keep to a word or two — a sentence becomes a wall.

Things to know before you commit

The trade-offs worth knowing

  • Search engines and site search may not match it. To a computer, 𝓱𝓮𝓵𝓵𝓸 is not the word hello. Keep your searchable text plain.
  • Screen readers handle it badly. Many announce the characters one by one or skip them, so avoid styling text that carries important information.
  • Some fonts lack the glyphs. Where a device has no glyph you get a box (□). Coverage is very good on modern phones and desktops, weaker on older devices and some smart TVs.
  • A few platforms filter it from names. Facebook and some games restrict which characters may appear in a real-name field.

None of that matters for a bio, a caption or a username. All of it matters for body text, headings on your own website, or anything you want found in search.

Frequently asked questions

What is square text?

Square text puts each letter inside a box using Unicode squared characters. There are outlined and filled variants.

Why does my square text sometimes show a flag?

Some squared letters share a Unicode block with regional indicator symbols, and certain pairs are interpreted as country flags. Change a letter or use the outlined style.

Are there squared numbers?

No. Unicode has no squared digits, so numbers keep your normal font.

Why do capitals and lowercase look the same?

The squared sets contain only one case, so both map to the same characters.

Does square text work in usernames?

Yes on most platforms. It is wide, so it may hit character limits faster than you expect.
