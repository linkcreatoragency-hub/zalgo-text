Light script or bold script?

Both come from the Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols block, and the difference is weight.

  • Bold Script (𝓒𝓾𝓻𝓼𝓲𝓿𝓮) — thicker strokes, far more legible on a phone, and the version most people mean when they say cursive. Use this one unless you have a reason not to.
  • Light Script (𝒞𝓊𝓇𝓈𝒾𝓋ℯ) — thin and delicate. Beautiful at large sizes, but strokes can disappear in a small comment thread or on a low-resolution screen.
Watch for the odd letter. The light script set borrows several capitals from an older Unicode block, so ℬ, ℰ, ℋ, ℐ, ℒ, ℳ and ℛ can look slightly out of step with their neighbours. Bold script has no such gaps.

How the letters are built

Why this is not really a font

Nothing here installs or downloads a font. These are separate Unicode characters that happen to look like styled versions of the Latin alphabet. Most of them live in the Mathematical Alphanumeric Symbols block, added so that a mathematician could write a bold vector and an italic variable and have the distinction survive in plain text.

Because they are characters rather than formatting, they travel anywhere text travels — including places that strip formatting entirely, like an Instagram bio or a Discord nickname. That is the whole trick.

Good uses for script letters

  • Profile names — script reads as personal and handmade, which suits creative accounts.
  • Quote captions — a styled quote with a plain attribution line underneath.
  • Wedding and event posts — script has an obvious formal-invitation association.
  • Signatures — style your name at the end of a long post.
  • Section headers in a bio — a script word, then plain lines beneath it.

Where it fails: anything long. Script is a display style, and a full paragraph of it is genuinely tiring to read.

Things to know before you commit

The trade-offs worth knowing

  • Search engines and site search may not match it. To a computer, 𝓱𝓮𝓵𝓵𝓸 is not the word hello. Keep your searchable text plain.
  • Screen readers handle it badly. Many announce the characters one by one or skip them, so avoid styling text that carries important information.
  • Some fonts lack the glyphs. Where a device has no glyph you get a box (□). Coverage is very good on modern phones and desktops, weaker on older devices and some smart TVs.
  • A few platforms filter it from names. Facebook and some games restrict which characters may appear in a real-name field.

None of that matters for a bio, a caption or a username. All of it matters for body text, headings on your own website, or anything you want found in search.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write in cursive on Instagram?

Type your text in the generator above, tap Copy next to Bold Script, then paste it into the Instagram bio or caption field.

Why does my cursive text show boxes on some phones?

That device's font has no glyph for those characters. Bold Script has better coverage than Light Script, so switch if you see boxes.

Is this a real cursive font?

No — it is a set of Unicode characters that look like cursive. That is why it works in apps that do not let you change fonts at all.

Can I use cursive text in my username?

On most platforms yes. Facebook and some games filter unusual characters out of name fields.

Why do some capitals look different in light script?

Seven capitals in that style come from an older Unicode block and were designed separately, so they do not match perfectly. Bold Script avoids the issue.
