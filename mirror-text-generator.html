Mirror text versus upside down text

They sound similar and are completely different transformations.

Mirror textUpside down text
TransformationReflected left ↔ rightRotated 180 degrees
Reading orderReversedReversed
Letter orientationBackwards, uprightInverted
ExampleɿoɿɿiMɹoɹɹıW
To read itHold up to a mirrorTurn the screen upside down

Mirror text is the harder of the two to fake, because far fewer Latin letters have a reversed twin in Unicode. Where none exists the original letter is kept, so mirrored text is usually a little more readable — and a little less convincing — than flipped text.

Which characters actually reverse

A handful of genuinely reversed letters do exist, mostly borrowed from Cyrillic and phonetic alphabets:

  • Я — Cyrillic ya, a perfect reversed R, and the single most recognisable mirror character. It is why so many band and film logos use it.
  • Ƨ, Ǝ, ᴎ — reversed S, E and N from phonetic and extended Latin blocks.
  • b ↔ d and p ↔ q — already mirror images of each other, so they simply swap.
  • Symmetrical letters — A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y need no substitution at all.

Everything else keeps its normal shape, which is a limitation of Unicode rather than of this tool.

What it is good for

  • Logos and band names — a single reversed letter is a decades-old design trick.
  • Puzzle posts — readable enough to be solvable, odd enough to make people stop.
  • Ambigram experiments — a starting point for testing whether a word can work reflected.
  • Mirror-selfie captions — matching the text to the photo.
  • Ingredient for cursed text — mirror first, then corrupt.

Things to know before you commit

The trade-offs worth knowing

  • Search engines and site search may not match it. To a computer, 𝓱𝓮𝓵𝓵𝓸 is not the word hello. Keep your searchable text plain.
  • Screen readers handle it badly. Many announce the characters one by one or skip them, so avoid styling text that carries important information.
  • Some fonts lack the glyphs. Where a device has no glyph you get a box (□). Coverage is very good on modern phones and desktops, weaker on older devices and some smart TVs.
  • A few platforms filter it from names. Facebook and some games restrict which characters may appear in a real-name field.

None of that matters for a bio, a caption or a username. All of it matters for body text, headings on your own website, or anything you want found in search.

Frequently asked questions

What is mirror text?

Mirror text is text reflected horizontally so it looks the way it would in a mirror. Letters are swapped for reversed lookalikes and the string is reversed.

How do I read mirrored text?

Hold the screen up to a mirror, or paste it back into the tool to reverse it again.

Why do some letters look normal?

Letters such as A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X and Y are symmetrical, and many others have no reversed form in Unicode, so they are left as they are.

Is mirror text the same as backwards text?

Close. Backwards text usually just reverses the character order; mirror text also swaps letters for reversed lookalikes where they exist.

Can I use mirror text in a username?

Yes on most platforms, though some filter unusual characters out of name fields.

Why is Я used so often in logos?

It is a Cyrillic letter that happens to be a perfect reversed R, which makes it the easiest way to suggest a mirror effect in a wordmark.
